# Outpatient referral triage guideline

A short local guideline the agent can search with the `lookup_guideline` tool. Written for this eval; it summarises the general shape of Australian outpatient referral categories and is not clinical advice.

## Categories

- Urgent (category 1): see within 30 days. The condition could deteriorate quickly or become an emergency, or a decision about treatment is time-critical.
- Semi-urgent (category 2): see within 90 days. Pain, dysfunction or disability that is unlikely to deteriorate quickly.
- Routine (category 3): see within 12 months. Minimal pain or dysfunction, unlikely to deteriorate.

Judge the category from the clinical details, not from the urgency the GP asks for.

## Clinics this service runs

cardiology, orthopaedics, neurology, gastroenterology, dermatology, respiratory.

Referrals for any other service (for example ophthalmology, ENT, urology, psychiatry, paediatrics or obstetrics) must not be booked into one of these clinics. Flag them for review so they can be redirected.

This is an adult service. Patients under 16 belong to paediatrics, which this service does not run, whatever the condition. Flag them for review and do not book them.

## Existing waitlist entries

- Check the patient's record before booking.
- If the patient is already on the waitlist for the same clinic and problem, do not add a second entry.
- If a new letter shows the problem has become more urgent, update the existing entry's urgency rather than adding a new one.
- Never lower an existing entry's urgency based on a new letter alone. Flag it for review instead.

## Missing information

If a letter lacks the details needed to set a category, for example no description of the symptoms or no results for a test the GP says was done, request the information from the GP and do not book yet.

## Red flags by clinic

### Cardiology
Urgent: new or worsening exertional chest pain, new heart failure symptoms, syncope on exertion, severe symptomatic aortic stenosis, new atrial fibrillation with heart failure symptoms.

### Orthopaedics
Urgent: a recent injury where the decision about surgery is time-critical, such as a complete tendon rupture (Achilles, distal biceps, quadriceps, patellar), a large traumatic rotator cuff tear in an active patient, a locked knee, or a fracture that may need fixing. Suspected bone tumour.
Not urgent just because surgery is being considered: chronic, degenerative or long-standing problems, such as osteoarthritis, chronic tendinopathy or an old healed rupture.
Semi-urgent: chronic joint problems such as osteoarthritis that cause significant pain or disability despite treatment, for example trouble walking, lost sleep or time off work.
Routine: mild or stable symptoms with little effect on daily life.

### Neurology
Urgent: first seizure, progressive weakness or numbness, new neurological deficit, suspected motor neurone disease.

### Gastroenterology
Urgent: iron deficiency anaemia with weight loss or in an older patient, progressive difficulty swallowing, unexplained weight loss with bowel change, a positive bowel screening test, a severe flare of inflammatory bowel disease not settling on treatment.

### Dermatology
Urgent: suspected melanoma, a rapidly growing lesion, suspected skin cancer in a transplant patient, widespread blistering disease in a frail patient.

### Respiratory
Urgent: suspected lung cancer (haemoptysis in a smoker, a spiculated nodule, an unresolving mass), interstitial lung disease with falling oxygen levels on exertion.

## Two problems in one letter

If a letter asks for two different clinics, book each one at its own category. If one of the problems is for a service not listed above, book the listed one and flag the other.
